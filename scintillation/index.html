    Scintillation and subaperture flux

    Since 2.3 a frame can carry a pupil-plane amplitude as well as the OPD, so the Shack-Hartmann and pyramid images include scintillation: an intensity pattern that changes from frame to frame. The Shack-Hartmann sensor also returns the flux of each subaperture, so the flux can be used as a measurement, and has two optional optical controls used in extreme-AO Shack-Hartmann designs: a focal-plane spatial filter in front of the lenslets and inter-lenslet cross-talk (no field stop between subapertures). ADR 0006 records why the amplitude is a per-frame input.

    Example

    import numpy as np
import makewfs

wfs = makewfs.WavefrontSensor.from_toml("examples/configs/shack_hartmann_scintillation.toml")
# U: complex pupil field at the sensing wavelength (from your propagation code)
# opd_m: the OPD your propagation tracks for that field (metres, input.shape)
amplitude = np.abs(U)  # relative to the unscintillated field
departure = np.angle(U * np.exp(-2j * np.pi * opd_m / wfs.config.sensor.wavelength_m))
opd_m = opd_m + departure * wfs.config.sensor.wavelength_m / (2 * np.pi)

frame = wfs.expose(opd_m, amplitude=amplitude, seed=0)  # noisy ADU frame
flux = wfs.subaperture_flux(opd_m, amplitude=amplitude)  # ideal, per lenslet
flux.detector_photons_s  # (n, n) photons/s in each subaperture's pixels
flux.pupil_photons_s  # (n, n) photons/s entering each lenslet
flux.valid  # (n, n) static valid-subaperture map

    examples/scintillation.py runs the whole chain: a pyturb phase screen at 12 km, Fresnel propagation to the pupil with aocore.AngularSpectrumPropagator, a deformable mirror emulated by removing the OPD below the lenslet Nyquist frequency, then the image, the subaperture fluxes, and illustrative centre-of-gravity slopes with and without the amplitude and behind a lambda / d spatial filter. With the default 16 x 16 configuration (scintillation index 0.36 at 700 nm) the pupil-plane subaperture flux correlates with the pupil intensity at 1.00, the flux in the 4 lambda / d detector windows at 0.60 (the window loses light that the 149 nm residual spreads beyond it), and the flux behind the spatial filter at 0.02.

    Pupil amplitude

    photon_rate, expose, expose_many (amplitudes=), expose_integrated, subaperture_flux, and simulate accept an optional amplitude. It is the dimensionless modulus of the incoming pupil field relative to the unscintillated field, A = |U| / |U_0|, and it multiplies the configured pupil amplitude. At wavelength lambda the field entering the sensor is

    pupil(x, y) * A_lambda(x, y) * exp(2 pi i OPD(x, y) / lambda)
    • Units and sign. A has no units and must be finite and non-negative; a complex array is rejected. The pupil intensity scales with A^2. Arrays are (y, x) like the OPD: a bright feature at +x lands at larger column index on the lenslet grid and in each of the four pyramid pupil images.
    • Normalization. The configured source rate (direct or from a magnitude) describes A = 1. A frame's photon rate is that rate times the pupil-weighted mean of A^2 (times the captured fraction), so scintillation moves the total flux as it does on the sky. Normalize a simulated field by its long-term mean intensity, not frame by frame, to keep that fluctuation.
    • Shapes. An input.shape map applies to every wavelength. A stack of shape (len(wfs.wavelengths_m), *input.shape) gives one map per wavelength node, in the order of WavefrontSensor.wavelengths_m (the spectral cube's order), for chromatic scintillation. expose_integrated takes one entry per temporal sample (a sequence, or an array whose leading axis is the sample axis).
    • Resampling. The map is defined on the input grid. When the propagation grid differs, its intensity is area-averaged onto the new cells with exact overlaps and the square root taken, so each cell receives the integral of the input intensity over it. A rotated or offset lenslet grid then samples it at the transformed coordinates by linear interpolation, as it does the OPD. Supplying the amplitude on the propagation grid itself (input.shape = lenslets_across_pupil * pupil_samples_per_lenslet) avoids any resampling.
    • Exactness. An amplitude of exactly one gives the OPD-only result bit for bit on the CPU, including resampled, rotated and offset grids, temporal integration, broadband and LGS quadrature, and the pyramid. On a GPU an amplitude routes a Shack-Hartmann frame to the array-operation reference path, because the compiled CUDA kernel takes the OPD only; that path agrees with the compiled one to rounding (2e-12 relative in float64 in the tests). A GPU pyramid replays a second CUDA graph for frames with an amplitude.
    • Metadata. Frames record wfs_input_amplitude (true when an amplitude was given). wfs_input_opd_rms_m and wfs_input_opd_rms_unweighted_m keep their definitions and use the configured pupil, not the amplitude.

    The OPD remains the only phase input because it is achromatic and can be interpolated without wrapping. A complex field at the sensing wavelength is represented exactly by A = |U| and an OPD whose phase equals arg U there, as in the example above: adding the small angle between U and the field of the OPD your propagation already tracks needs no phase unwrapping. Supply such an OPD on the propagation grid, since linearly interpolating it across a 2 pi jump of that angle would be wrong. At other wavelengths of a broadband source the phase is then 2 pi OPD / lambda, which neglects the chromatic part of the diffractive phase.

    Subaperture flux

    WavefrontSensor.subaperture_flux(wavefront, amplitude=None, offset_state=0) renders one frame and returns a SubapertureFlux with (lenslet_y, lenslet_x) arrays on the sensor's device:

    Field Meaning (photons/s, ideal)
    detector_photons_s Photon rate collected by each subaperture's own pixels_per_subaperture square block of pixels: the block sum of photon_rate without the detector margin. It loses what the spot sends beyond the block and, with cross-talk, gains what neighbours send in.
    pupil_photons_s Photon rate entering each lenslet's clear aperture: the source rate times the integral of the entrance-field intensity over the lenslet (after the spatial filter and the lenslet fill factor), divided by the unscintillated pupil's integral. It does not depend on the detector.
    valid The static valid-subaperture map of the offset state.
    photon_rate The ideal image the detector flux was summed from.

    For a uniform pupil A = 1 the pupil_photons_s sum to the source rate. Both fluxes are noise-free. A measured flux is the same block sum of an exposed frame (m is detector_margin_pixels, p the pixels per subaperture, n the lenslets across):

    data = np.asarray(frame.data, dtype=float)[m : m + n * p, m : m + n * p]
measured = data.reshape(n, p, n, p).sum(axis=(1, 3))

    Dividing the measured flux by that of a flat-field (amplitude-one) frame removes the static pattern of partly illuminated and edge subapertures.

    Focal-plane spatial filter

    [shack_hartmann]
spatial_filter_width_lambda_over_d = 1.0

    A square stop in a focal plane in front of the lenslet array, as in extreme-AO Shack-Hartmann sensors (L. A. Poyneer and B. Macintosh, Spatially filtered wave-front sensor for high-order adaptive optics, JOSA A 21, 810, 2004), low-passes the whole pupil field before the lenslets sample it. Its full width is c lambda_ref / d: c is the key, lambda_ref is sensor.wavelength_m, and d is the lenslet pitch on the pupil (input.grid_extent_m / lenslets_across_pupil). c = 1 passes exactly the spatial frequencies the lenslets can sample without aliasing.

    • The stop passes the pupil-plane spatial frequencies |f_x|, |f_y| < c / (2 d) * lambda_ref / lambda: it is a fixed angle, so a longer wavelength passes fewer cycles across the pupil. A frequency exactly on the edge is blocked.
    • The entrance field (pupil times amplitude times phase, in the lenslet frame, so the square is aligned with the lenslets) is zero-padded to twice its size, Fourier transformed, restricted to the passband, transformed back, and cropped to the lenslet array. Only the passband lines are computed. The lenslet fill-factor mask applies after the filter.
    • The stop is centred on the optical axis. An off-axis or finite source is filtered off-centre, as it would be by a physical stop.
    • Light the stop removes, and light it diffracts beyond the lenslet array, is lost and reported in wfs_captured_photons_s and pupil_photons_s; nothing is renormalized.
    • The filter turns residual phase into intensity: a phase ripple beyond the cutoff loses its diffracted orders, so the lenslet flux drops by 1 - J0(a)^2 for a ripple of a radians (checked in the tests). Behind the filter the subaperture flux therefore measures the residual phase as much as the scintillation.
    • It is not available with range-resolved LGS states (source.lgs_ranges_m), whose per-subaperture tilts are not one pupil field a stop could act on.

    Inter-lenslet cross-talk

    Without cross-talk each lenslet's spot is propagated, integrated, and cropped to its own pixels_per_subaperture window, which acts as a field stop at the window: light beyond it is lost (subaperture_field_of_view_arcsec() reports the window's field). An instrument without such a stop lets that light fall on the neighbouring subapertures' pixels:

    [shack_hartmann]
crosstalk_reach_subapertures = 1
detector_margin_pixels = 8      # optional: keep the spill of edge lenslets

    With a reach k every lenslet is propagated over a window 2k + 1 subaperture windows wide on each axis, centred on its own, and every part of it is added into the pixels it falls on. The image is the overlap-add of those windows at the lenslet pitch (checked against independently propagated wide windows in the tests). The total detected light equals the light inside each lenslet's widened window while it stays on the detector; what falls beyond the reach or off the detector (including any margin) is lost and reported. A flat-wavefront square lenslet keeps (2/pi Si(2 pi a))^2 of its light within +-a lambda/d: 0.902 in a 4 lambda/d window, 0.967 with k = 1 around it.

    The lenslet field is sampled finely enough for the widened window not to alias (samples_per_lenslet >= (2k + 1) * pixels / sampling), so a reach raises the propagation cost. A configured circular field stop is applied over the widened window. Cross-talk is not available with source.lgs_model = "kernel"; the range quadrature model supports it.

    Limitations

    • makewfs does not propagate the field: the amplitude and the OPD describe the field at the pupil, computed by the caller's atmosphere and propagation code.
    • One OPD serves every wavelength: per-wavelength amplitudes are supported, per-wavelength phases are not.
    • Overlapping light from neighbouring lenslets is added as intensity. The coherent interference between neighbouring spots, which depends on their relative phase and washes out over a spectral band or a finite source, is not modelled.
    • The spatial filter is square, centred on the axis, and uses a fixed two-fold zero padding.
    • On a GPU, frames with an amplitude, a spatial filter, or cross-talk use the array-operation Shack-Hartmann path rather than the compiled kernel (about 3 ms instead of 0.7 ms per frame for a 20 x 20 float32 sensor on a Quadro P620; see Performance).
    • subaperture_flux is Shack-Hartmann only. The pyramid takes an amplitude but has no subapertures.
